The definition of the derivative

Slope at a point

A line has the same slope everywhere. A curve does not. A slope needs two points, so what could the slope of a curve at a single point mean?

Take the point P=(a,f(a)) and a second point Q=(a+h,\,f(a+h)) on the curve. The ochre secant line through P and Q has a slope you can compute. Drag Q toward P. What happens to that slope as h shrinks?

Reading the definition

The derivative of f at a is

f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}

provided this limit exists. Each part matches something in the graph above.

f(a+h)-f(a)
The rise: how much the output changes between P and Q.
h
The run: how far Q is from P horizontally. It may be positive or negative, but it is never 0, since dividing by 0 is undefined.
The quotient
The slope of the secant line through P and Q. This is an ordinary slope between two points.
\lim_{h\to 0}
The value the secant slopes approach as Q slides toward P from either side. If the slopes from the left and from the right approach the same number, that number is the slope of the tangent line.

Writing x=a+h gives an equivalent form that some texts prefer:

f'(a)=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}

Because f'(a) is a limit, it carries the ε-δ meaning: for every \varepsilon>0 there is a \delta>0 such that 0<|h|<\delta puts the difference quotient within \varepsilon of f'(a).

Computing a derivative from the definition

Setting h=0 in the difference quotient always gives \tfrac{0}{0}, which tells you nothing. The work is to simplify the quotient until the factor of h in the denominator cancels. Try each step yourself before revealing it.

      When there is no derivative

      The limit has to exist from both sides and agree. For f(x)=|x| at a=0, the difference quotient is \tfrac{|h|}{h}. What is it when h>0? When h<0? The two one-sided limits are 1 and -1, so |x| has no derivative at 0. The graph has a corner there.

      For f(x)=\sqrt{x} at a=0, only h>0 makes sense, and the quotient is \tfrac{\sqrt h}{h}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt h}. It grows without bound as h\to 0^+. The secant lines approach a vertical line, which has no slope.
