The limit laws

Taking limits apart

If f(x)\to 3 and g(x)\to -1 as x\to 1, what does f(x)+g(x) approach? It seems obvious. When does that kind of reasoning break down?

The limit laws say you may take a limit apart, term by term or factor by factor, provided each piece has a limit and you never divide by a limit of 0. Build a combination below and compare what the laws predict with what the function actually does.

The limit laws

Suppose \lim_{x\to a}f(x) and \lim_{x\to a}g(x) both exist, and c is a constant. Then:

LawStatement
Sum and difference\lim\,[f(x)\pm g(x)]=\lim f(x)\pm\lim g(x)
Constant multiple\lim\,c\,f(x)=c\lim f(x)
Product\lim\,f(x)g(x)=\lim f(x)\cdot\lim g(x)
Quotient\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\dfrac{\lim f(x)}{\lim g(x)}\ \text{ if } \lim g(x)\ne 0
Power and root\lim\,[f(x)]^n=\big[\lim f(x)\big]^n,\quad \lim\sqrt[n]{f(x)}=\sqrt[n]{\lim f(x)}

For a root with n even, the limit of f(x) must be positive. Every law has the same condition: the pieces must have limits. The laws say nothing when a piece has no limit. In that case the combination might still have one. Try the first button in the graph: \sin\tfrac{1}{x-1} has no limit, yet adding its negative gives exactly 0.

Direct substitution

Applying the laws again and again shows that if p is a polynomial, then \lim_{x\to a}p(x)=p(a). The same holds for a rational function p/q as long as q(a)\ne 0. That is why substituting is always the first thing to try. Why does it fail when q(a)=0?

    Common mistakes

    Using the quotient law when the bottom approaches 0

    If \lim g(x)=0, the quotient law does not apply. You need another method, such as factoring, or the conclusion that no finite limit exists.

    Concluding too much from a failed law

    If \lim f(x) does not exist, that does not prove \lim\,[f(x)+g(x)] fails to exist. The law is silent, not negative.

    Splitting when a piece has no limit

    Writing \lim x\sin\tfrac1x=\lim x\cdot\lim\sin\tfrac1x is invalid, because the second limit does not exist. The squeeze theorem handles this one.
